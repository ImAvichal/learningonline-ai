# Parents & Caregivers — Practical AI Learning Refresh (Draft)
**Last reviewed:** 23 November 2026 (Australia/Sydney)
**Status:** CONTENT DRAFT ONLY — not approved for publication; no school endorsement claimed
**Audience:** Australian parents/carers of school-age children, especially upper-primary and secondary years
**Scope:** Supplemental content to review against the nine existing /parents lessons. Do not overwrite lessons without editorial QA.

## Learning promise
In 30–45 minutes, a parent can explain what generative AI does, agree on age-appropriate family boundaries, support learning without outsourcing thinking, protect privacy, spot manipulated content, and know when a child needs a trusted human.

## Six high-priority lesson enhancements

### 1. Start with curiosity, not surveillance (5 minutes)
**Parent script:** “What AI tools have you seen your friends use? Show me something interesting they can do. What do you think they get wrong?”
**Try together:** Ask an AI tool to explain why the sky appears blue. Compare its explanation against a trusted science source. Identify one claim that needs checking.
**Key point:** Chatbots generate plausible responses; apparent confidence does not establish accuracy.
**Safeguard:** Do not require children to sign up to a consumer AI service to complete this activity. A parent may demonstrate using an account they are authorised to use, or use a teacher-approved tool.

### 2. Homework: coach, don't ghostwrite (7 minutes)
**Allowed-use examples to discuss with the teacher:** Ask for hints, a worked example on a different problem, feedback on the student's own draft, or a quiz on material already studied.
**Usually inappropriate without teacher permission:** Submitting AI-written answers as original work, uploading a classmate's work, fabricating references, or using AI on restricted assessments.
**Practice:** Take a fictional Year 7 maths problem. Ask for three hints, not the answer; solve it on paper; then explain the reasoning without the chatbot.
**Family rule:** “I can explain the answer myself, and I follow the school's assessment policy.” School policies take precedence.

### 3. Privacy before prompts (6 minutes)
**Never paste into unapproved AI tools:** Full names plus identifying details, school timetables, student IDs, passwords, addresses, private messages, health or counselling details, photos of classmates, or confidential school documents.
**Practice:** Transform “Please analyse Sam Patel's Year 7 report from [school], with their date of birth and grades” into “Suggest ways a fictional Year 7 student could improve study habits.”
**Family rule:** Ask permission before sharing anyone else's image, voice or information. Check product-specific age rules and parental consent before creating accounts.

### 4. AI companions and emotional safety (7 minutes)
A friendly chatbot is not a trusted adult, therapist or replacement for friends. Some children ask AI about relationships, distress or sensitive issues; respond without shame.
**Parent script:** “If a chatbot tells you to keep a secret, makes you uncomfortable, or gives advice about your safety or health, show me or another trusted adult. You won't be in trouble for asking for help.”
**Practice:** Compare a fictional chatbot's reassuring but unverified advice with a response that encourages talking to a trusted person.
**Boundary:** Keep real-world relationships and offline activities central. For immediate danger in Australia, call 000.

### 5. Deepfakes, false confidence and checking sources (6 minutes)
**Practice:** Use an openly labelled, synthetic example of an AI-generated headline or image. Ask: Who created it? Can I find an independent original source? Is the date current? Is the evidence verifiable?
**Family rule:** Don't forward sensational media until checked; never generate or share intimate, humiliating or impersonating images of real people.

### 6. A family AI agreement (5 minutes)
Complete together and revisit monthly:
- We use age-appropriate, school-approved tools and follow each provider's minimum-age/consent rules.
- AI may explain, quiz or brainstorm; we do our own learning and disclose AI assistance when required.
- We keep private information and other people's images out of unapproved services.
- We check consequential claims against reliable sources.
- We tell a trusted adult about uncomfortable, manipulative or harmful interactions.
- We make time for human conversations, play, reading and independent thinking.

## Printable parent checklist
- [ ] I know which AI tools my child uses and their account/age requirements.
- [ ] I have read the school's homework and assessment guidance.
- [ ] We practised one “hint, don't answer” learning exercise.
- [ ] We discussed personal data, photos and deepfakes.
- [ ] My child knows which adults they can approach if AI advice feels unsafe.
- [ ] We agreed to revisit our family rules in four weeks.

## School-community use (optional, not school-endorsed)
**20-minute parent introduction:** 5 min AI basics; 5 min homework demo; 5 min privacy/companions/deepfakes; 5 min questions and family agreement.
**Do not claim:** school partnership, official endorsement, guaranteed learning outcomes, professional counselling, or that any consumer tool is suitable for all ages.
**Feedback prompt:** “Which topic would help your family most: homework, privacy, AI companions, deepfakes, or school rules?” Collect only with appropriate consent and approved privacy notices.

## Editorial corrections for the existing nine lessons
1. Replace categorical “AI cannot know whether a statement is true” with “AI can check some claims using tools or sources, but can still be wrong; verify consequential information independently.”
2. Label all illustrative family anecdotes as **fictional examples** unless verifiable consent and evidence exist.
3. Remove unsupported prevalence claims such as “parents rarely know” and unverified age-by-activity tables; cite research when using statistics.
4. Avoid instructing every child to create a chatbot account. State minimum-age, consent and school-approval requirements.
5. Distinguish AI assistants from AI companions; discuss emotional dependency without implying all interactions are harmful.
6. Avoid absolute claims about data training or privacy; refer to the exact provider, product, account settings and current policy.
7. Include a clear escalation path for safety concerns and human review.
8. Avoid provider/version lists unless dated and checked against primary sources.
